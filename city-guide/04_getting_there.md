Getting There

### By Air

San Francisco is served by three airports.

**San Francisco International Airport (SFO)** is the primary gateway, located about 14 miles south of downtown. It has direct connections to most major US cities and a large number of international destinations across Europe, Asia, and Latin America. BART (the regional rail system) connects SFO directly to downtown San Francisco in about 30 minutes for around $10. Taxis and rideshare are more expensive but faster during off-peak hours.

**Oakland International Airport (OAK)** is across the bay and often offers cheaper fares, particularly on Southwest and budget carriers. BART connects Oakland's Coliseum station to the airport via a connector; from there, downtown San Francisco is about 25 minutes on the train.

**San Jose International Airport (SJC)** is the furthest away — about 50 miles south. It makes sense if you are spending time in the South Bay. Getting to San Francisco requires either a rental car or a combination of Caltrain and BART.

### By Train

Amtrak does not serve San Francisco directly. The California Zephyr and Coast Starlight terminate in Emeryville, across the bay, with bus connections to the Ferry Building or other downtown points. Caltrain connects San Francisco (at 4th and King Streets) to the Peninsula and the South Bay, including downtown San Jose.

### By Car

San Francisco is connected to the broader Bay Area by several bridges. The Bay Bridge links the city to Oakland and the East Bay. The Golden Gate Bridge leads north to Marin County. Driving into the city is possible but parking is expensive, scarce, and stressful. If you are arriving by car, consider parking in a residential neighborhood further from downtown and using transit for most of your time in the city.

### By Bus

Greyhound and FlixBus serve the Salesforce Transit Center in downtown San Francisco with connections across California and beyond. Megabus offers routes to Los Angeles and other California cities.
