Festivals & Annual Events

San Francisco's calendar is full. The city's tolerance for elaborate public expression, combined with its clustering of cultural institutions, produces an event calendar of unusual density.

### Chinese New Year Parade (January/February)

The oldest and largest Chinese New Year celebration in the United States, running since 1860. The parade through Chinatown and downtown includes a 175-foot golden dragon and draws large crowds. Usually the second or third weekend after the lunar new year.

### SF International Film Festival (April/May)

The oldest film festival in the Americas, running since 1957, screens around 150 films from 50 countries over two weeks. Venues include the Castro Theatre and the Alamo Drafthouse.

### Bay to Breakers (May)

A 12K footrace from the Embarcadero to Ocean Beach that has been run since 1912. It is as much a costumed street party as a race. Some participants wear extraordinary costumes. Some wear nothing at all. The floats are a tradition.

### Carnaval San Francisco (May)

The Mission District's Carnaval, held on Memorial Day weekend, is the largest Carnaval in the western United States, celebrating Latin American and Caribbean cultures with music, dancing, and elaborate costumes.

### SF Pride (June)

One of the world's largest Pride celebrations. The parade runs down Market Street on the last Sunday of June; the Civic Center Plaza hosts two days of stages, vendors, and community organizations.

### Outside Lands (August)

A major outdoor music festival in Golden Gate Park, held over a weekend in August. Headliners in recent years have included major names across rock, hip-hop, and electronic music. The food and wine programming is equally serious.

### Hardly Strictly Bluegrass (September/October)

A free, outdoor music festival in Golden Gate Park dedicated to roots music in the broadest sense. Three days, six stages, over 100 performers, and no admission charge — funded by the estate of the late investment manager and music lover Warren Hellman.

### Folsom Street Fair (September)

The world's largest leather and kink street fair, held on the last Sunday of September in SoMa. Not for the easily surprised.

### Fleet Week (October)

The US Navy and Marine Corps visit the city each October for Fleet Week, with ship tours at the waterfront and air shows featuring the Blue Angels. The Blue Angels performances, flying low over the bay, are genuinely spectacular.

### Día de los Muertos (November 1-2)

The Mission District's Day of the Dead celebration is one of the most significant in the United States, with community altars, processions, face painting, and events centered on Garfield Park and the streets of the Mission.
