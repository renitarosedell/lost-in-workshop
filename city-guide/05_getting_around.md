Getting Around the City

San Francisco is a compact city, but its hills and variable weather make a few things worth knowing before you navigate it.

### BART (Bay Area Rapid Transit)

BART is the regional rail system connecting San Francisco to the East Bay, the Peninsula, and SFO. Within the city itself, BART runs along Market Street underground with stations at Embarcadero, Montgomery, Powell, Civic Center, 16th Street Mission, 24th Street Mission, and Glen Park. It is fast, clean, and reliable for getting between these specific points, but limited in its coverage of non-Market Street neighborhoods.

### Muni

The San Francisco Municipal Railway (Muni) operates the city's internal transit network, including buses, light rail (the Muni Metro), and the historic cable cars. The network is comprehensive but can be slow. The SFMTA app or Google Maps are your best planning tools.

**Cable Cars** are a legitimate way to travel and not just a tourist attraction, but they are slow, crowded, and run only on three lines. The Powell-Hyde line is the most scenic. Buy a single ride or use a Clipper Card.

### Rideshare and Taxis

Uber and Lyft are widely available and often the most practical option for short hops in neighborhoods poorly served by transit, or late at night. Taxis exist but are harder to hail on the street.

### Walking

San Francisco is one of the world's great walking cities, but you have to earn it. Hills are steep — Filbert Street between Hyde and Leavenworth has a 31.5% grade. Wear comfortable shoes, plan your routes to avoid unnecessary climbs, and build in time to stop and look around.

### Cycling

The city has expanded its bike infrastructure considerably, but cycling is not for the faint-hearted on many streets. The waterfront path from the Ferry Building to Crissy Field and beyond is excellent. Bay Wheels (Ford GoBike) is the bikeshare system; electric assist options are available.

### Ferry

The Golden Gate Ferry and WETA (Water Emergency Transportation Authority) run services to Sausalito, Tiburon, Larkspur, Vallejo, and other bay destinations from the Ferry Building. A ferry to Sausalito or Tiburon makes for a scenic and practical alternative to the bridge.
