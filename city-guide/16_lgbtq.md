LGBTQ+ San Francisco

San Francisco's LGBTQ+ community has shaped the city's identity as deeply as any other force in its modern history.

### The Castro

The Castro neighborhood has been the geographic and symbolic heart of LGBTQ+ San Francisco since the 1970s. Harvey Milk, the first openly gay elected official in California, ran his camera shop and his political campaigns from Castro Street. His assassination in 1978, along with Mayor George Moscone, became a galvanizing moment in the history of LGBTQ+ rights in America.

The Castro Theatre, opened in 1922, is a magnificent movie palace that has hosted film festivals, community events, and performances for over a century. The rainbow flag crosswalks and the Human Rights Campaign Action Center are visible symbols of the neighborhood's identity.

### The GLBT History Museum

On Castro Street, the GLBT History Museum is the first fully dedicated LGBTQ+ history museum in the United States, documenting the history of the community in San Francisco and beyond with archival photographs, documents, and personal testimonies.

### The AIDS Memorial

The AIDS Memorial Grove in Golden Gate Park is a national memorial to those who died in the AIDS epidemic, which devastated San Francisco's gay community through the 1980s and early 1990s and spurred an extraordinary activist response. The Names Project AIDS Memorial Quilt began in San Francisco.

### Pride

San Francisco Pride, held in late June, is one of the oldest and largest Pride celebrations in the world. The parade down Market Street draws hundreds of thousands of participants and spectators.

### SoMa & Leather Culture

The SoMa neighborhood south of Market Street has historically been home to the leather and kink communities, with bars and clubs that have been part of the city's LGBTQ+ fabric for decades. The Folsom Street Fair, held annually in September, is the world's largest leather event.
