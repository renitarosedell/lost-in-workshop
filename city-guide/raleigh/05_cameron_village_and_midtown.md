# Cameron Village & Midtown

Cameron Village holds a modest but significant place in American urban history: when it
opened in 1949 it was one of the first planned regional shopping centres built in the
American South, and one of the earliest anywhere to be designed explicitly for the
automobile era — surface parking surrounded the shops on three sides. Seventy-five years
later, the parking has not gone anywhere, but the shopping centre itself has evolved into
a pleasant open-air district with a mix of national retailers, local independents,
restaurants, and a genuine neighbourhood feel that most strip malls never achieved.

The site was developed on the former estate of George Badger, a nineteenth-century
senator and US Supreme Court nominee whose family farm occupied the land west of downtown.
The Cameron name persists in the development and in the adjacent Cameron Park
neighbourhood, a quiet residential district of early twentieth-century houses bounded by
St. Mary's Street, Cameron Avenue, Oberlin Road, and Wade Avenue.

**What Cameron Village offers today** is a walkable open-air retail cluster anchored by a
Harris Teeter grocery store, a branch of the Durham County Library, a handful of
restaurants (sushi, pizza, burgers, a proper bakery), and a collection of independent
shops — a running store, a kitchen shop, a children's toy shop — that have no obvious
reason to survive in the age of e-commerce yet somehow do.

The district's most notable restaurant cluster sits at the western edge, along Clark
Avenue and at the corner of Morrison Drive: Bida Manda, The Pit, Mecca Restaurant (a
downtown institution relocated here), and several bars that cater to the NC State student
population flowing in from the adjacent Hillsborough Street corridor.

**Hillsborough Street** runs east–west along the northern edge of NC State University's
campus and connects Cameron Village (on the west end) to the Beltline (on the east end)
through a strip of mid-century commercial buildings, student-oriented restaurants, and
bars. It is one of the livelier pedestrian streets in the city and has seen significant
investment in streetscaping over the past decade.

**Getting to Cameron Village** from downtown: GoRaleigh Route 11 runs from Moore Square
along Glenwood Avenue and has stops near the Village District at the edge of Cameron
Village. Rideshare from Moore Square is eight to ten minutes. Bikeshare from Glenwood
South is approximately twelve minutes via Oberlin Road.

**For workshop context**: Cameron Village represents the city's mid-century westward
expansion. Understanding where Cameron Village is helps situate the west side of Raleigh:
it sits roughly one mile west of downtown, between the rail corridor and the NC State
campus, and is bounded to the north by the Wade Avenue connector, one of the primary
routes to Research Triangle Park.

The NC Museum of Art, located on Blue Ridge Road a mile further west, is technically
part of the Cameron Village / Midtown axis — a cluster of cultural and civic institutions
that stretches from the university campus out to the Beltline. Midtown itself is a looser
commercial zone along Atlantic Avenue, north of I-440, where chain retail and newer
mixed-use development have taken hold alongside older strip malls.
