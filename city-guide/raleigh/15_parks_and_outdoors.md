# Parks & Outdoors: Pullen Park & Umstead State Park

Raleigh's outdoor offerings range from small urban pocket parks to one of the most
accessible state parks in North Carolina, and understanding the park system helps
you appreciate how the city thinks about its relationship with the natural landscape.

**Pullen Park** is Raleigh's oldest public park, opened in 1887, and it occupies a
special place in the city's social history. Located on Ashe Avenue near the NC State
University campus, it covers 68 acres of gently rolling terrain beside Pullen Road
and the rail corridor. It is best known for three things:

*The carousel*: One of only five Dentzel carousels still operating in the United States,
installed at Pullen Park in 1921. It is a genuine antique, beautifully maintained, and
rides cost a dollar. If you have children with you, this is non-negotiable.

*Paddle boats*: The park has a lake where you can rent paddleboats by the hour. On a warm
afternoon, this is one of the more pleasant ways to spend an hour in Raleigh.

*The Pullen Aquatic Center*: A public indoor pool complex that is popular with the
university community and neighbourhood families.

The park also has tennis courts, a kiddie train ride (seasonal), picnic shelters, and
playgrounds. It is a genuine working public park that feels like it belongs to the
people who use it rather than to Instagram.

**William B. Umstead State Park** is the outdoor jewel of the Raleigh area, an 5,579-
acre forested park on the western edge of Raleigh (technically split between Wake and
Durham counties) that sits astonishingly close to the urban core given its size. The main
entrances are at 8801 Glenwood Avenue (the Crabtree Creek section) and at 682 Ebenezer
Church Road (the Reedy Creek section). Both are accessible by car; the Crabtree Creek
section is about 15 minutes from downtown by rideshare.

Umstead offers:
- 22 miles of hiking trails through mixed hardwood and pine forests.
- 13 miles of equestrian trails (horses from approved private stables).
- 2 miles of paved multi-use trail for cycling and running.
- Three fishing lakes (no motorized boats; fishing license required).
- A family campground (reservations required).

The park's forest feels surprisingly wild for something so close to a major American city.
The Sycamore Trail and Company Mill Trail are the most popular hiking routes, winding
through stream valleys with good bird-watching (especially warblers in spring).

For workshop participants who finish early or want a recovery walk: Umstead is worth the
short rideshare from downtown. It offers a genuine escape from the conference room and
provides context for what the Research Triangle landscape looked like before the
universities and office parks arrived.
