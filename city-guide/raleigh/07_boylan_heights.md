# Boylan Heights

Boylan Heights is one of Raleigh's oldest and most architecturally intact residential
neighbourhoods, occupying a hillside south and west of the Warehouse District, between
South West Street, South Saunders Street, Western Boulevard, and the rail corridor. It was
developed between 1907 and 1930 as a streetcar suburb, with the original lots laid out to
appeal to middle-class families who wanted a quieter life within walking distance of
downtown.

The neighbourhood's defining feature is its housing stock: a remarkably well-preserved
collection of craftsman bungalows, foursquare houses, and Victorian cottages from the
first three decades of the twentieth century. The streets are narrow and shaded, the lots
generous by downtown standards, and the sidewalks uneven in a way that signals age rather
than neglect. Boylan Heights is on the National Register of Historic Places.

**Boylan Avenue Bridge** is the neighbourhood's most famous landmark — a concrete span
from the 1910s that crosses the rail corridor and offers what many consider the best
single view of the Raleigh skyline: the office towers and State Capitol dome framed by the
oak canopy of the neighbourhood behind you and the rail lines below. Photographers and
joggers favour it at golden hour.

The neighbourhood is entirely residential except for a small commercial cluster at the
corner of South Saunders and Western Boulevard. There are no restaurants or bars within
Boylan Heights itself (a deliberate outcome of its historic zoning), but the Warehouse
District is a ten-to-fifteen minute walk northeast, and South Saunders Street connects
south to the growing South Park neighbourhood.

**Community life** in Boylan Heights is anchored by the Boylan Heights Neighborhood
Association, one of the oldest and most active in the city. The association publishes a
neighbourhood newsletter and maintains a community garden on South Boylan Avenue. Annual
events include a porch parade at Halloween, a neighbourhood yard sale in spring, and an
informal holiday lights competition.

**Getting to Boylan Heights** from the Warehouse District is a pleasant uphill walk of
eight to ten minutes (uphill toward the bridge, which is how you know you are entering
the neighbourhood). Rideshare is available but drivers sometimes have trouble with the
narrow one-way streets in the interior; being specific about your drop-off address helps.

For workshop participants navigating the Warehouse Run quest: the handoff point in Boylan
Heights is at the neighbourhood's eastern edge, near the Boylan Avenue Bridge. The view
from the bridge looking back toward downtown is worth the climb even on a tight schedule.

The neighbourhood's quiet streets and craftsman porches feel far removed from the pace of
downtown — a reminder that Raleigh has always been a city of distinct layers, where you
can shift from breweries and art galleries to century-old residential calm within the space
of a ten-minute walk.
