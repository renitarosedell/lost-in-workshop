# Getting Around: GoRaleigh Buses

GoRaleigh is Raleigh's municipal bus system, operated by the City of Raleigh's transit
department. It is not a world-class transit system by the standards of Chicago or San
Francisco, but it is serviceable for reaching major destinations from downtown, and for
workshop participants navigating between quest locations it is a genuinely useful option.

**The hub: Moore Square Transit Center.** Almost every GoRaleigh route passes through or
originates at the Moore Square Transit Center on Blount Street between Hargett and Martin
streets, immediately adjacent to Moore Square park. If you are unsure which bus to take,
go to Moore Square and ask the staff at the transit shelter, or check the digital displays
that show real-time arrivals for each route.

**Fares.** A single GoRaleigh ride costs $1.25. You can pay with exact change in coins or
with the GoTriangle Go! app, which loads a digital pass onto your phone. Day passes cost
$2.50 and allow unlimited GoRaleigh rides. The bus does not give change, so bring
quarters. Most routes accept GoTriangle regional fare cards as well.

**Key routes for quest participants:**

*Route 11 (Glenwood Avenue)*: Departs Moore Square Transit Center, runs north along
Fayetteville Street and then Glenwood Avenue through downtown, past Peace Street, through
Glenwood South, to Five Points and beyond. Frequency: every 30 minutes during day service
(roughly 6am–10pm on weekdays). Journey time from Moore Square to Glenwood South: ~18
minutes.

*Route 16 (Hillsborough Street)*: Runs from downtown west along Hillsborough Street to
NC State University and beyond. Useful for reaching the Village District and Cameron
Village area. Frequency: every 30 minutes.

*Route 5 (Six Forks Road)*: Runs from downtown north along Six Forks Road to the North
Hills area. Useful if you need to reach the north side of the city.

*Route 2 (South Saunders)*: Runs south from downtown along South Saunders Street,
passing near Boylan Heights.

**Real-time tracking.** The GoRaleigh website and the Google Maps transit layer both show
real-time bus locations. The GoTriangle Go! app is the most reliable for real-time arrival
predictions. Buses in Raleigh generally run on schedule outside of peak congestion hours
(roughly 7:30–9:00am and 4:30–6:30pm on weekdays).

**Practical notes.**
- Buses kneel for accessibility; all buses have bicycle racks on the front (two-bike capacity).
- GoRaleigh does not run 24 hours; last buses on most routes are around 10pm weekdays and
  11pm on weekends. Check the schedule before planning a late-night trip.
- Standing at the stop and signalling the driver (raising your hand or stepping toward the
  street) ensures the bus pulls over, particularly at less-busy stops.

GoRaleigh is the cheapest and often most reliable way to move between Moore Square and
Glenwood South, and between downtown and the museum district on Bicentennial Plaza.
For most other quest legs, rideshare or bike are faster.
