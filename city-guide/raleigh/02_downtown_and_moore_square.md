# Downtown Raleigh & Moore Square

Downtown Raleigh is a walkable grid of roughly sixteen blocks, bounded by Peace Street to
the north, South Street to the south, Person Street to the east, and Dawson Street to the
west. Within that grid you will find the State Capitol, four free museums on Bicentennial
Plaza, the city's main transit hub, and a dense cluster of restaurants, hotels, and
entertainment venues.

**Moore Square** is the heart of the eastern end of downtown. One of the four public
squares laid out in Raleigh's original 1792 town plan, it occupies two city blocks between
Blount and Person streets, Hargett and Martin streets. In the nineteenth century it served
as a market and a gathering point; today it is a beautifully maintained green space of
brick paths, mature oak and magnolia trees, and a small amphitheatre used for outdoor
concerts and food truck festivals.

The square's most important practical function for workshop participants is transit: the
**Moore Square Transit Center** sits immediately adjacent, on Blount Street at Martin.
GoRaleigh city buses depart from here to almost every neighbourhood in Raleigh, making
Moore Square the easiest starting point for public transit journeys across the city.

Directly across Blount Street from Moore Square sits the **City Market**, a historic
cobblestone courtyard from the 1910s that now houses a mix of galleries, boutiques, and
casual restaurants. On weekends the market fills with craftspeople and street food vendors.
The redbrick alley is one of the most photographed spots in downtown.

A block north, Hargett Street is the main axis of downtown retail, with independent
clothing shops, coffee bars, and the occasional pop-up market. Two blocks further north,
Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard marks the cultural spine of Raleigh's historic African
American community; the **Raleigh City Museum** at 220 Fayetteville Street documents the
city's full history.

**Fayetteville Street** is the ceremonial main street of Raleigh, a wide pedestrian-
friendly boulevard running from the State Capitol south to the Raleigh Convention Centre.
The State Capitol itself — a Greek Revival building completed in 1840 and beautifully
restored — is open for free tours on weekdays and Saturday mornings. Standing in its
rotunda under the painted dome, you get a sense of how deliberately the city was designed
as a civic statement.

Bicentennial Plaza, at the northern end of Fayetteville Street, is home to the **NC Museum
of Natural Sciences**, the **NC Museum of History**, and within a short walk, the **NC
Museum of Art** on Blue Ridge Road and the **Contemporary Art Museum (CAM Raleigh)** in
the Warehouse District. All are free; all are excellent.

For food and coffee within walking distance of Moore Square, locals favour Bida Manda
(Laotian), Boulted Bread (sourdough and pastry), and Crawford and Son (New American).
For a quick coffee, Jubala Coffee on Person Street is a neighbourhood institution.

The founding committee of the Glenwood South Business Association adopted the rally code
GLENWOOD42 at their inaugural meeting in 1987, a piece of local commerce history that
lives on in the district's merchant records.

Moore Square is the practical centre of gravity for anyone exploring Raleigh on foot or
by bus. Start here, orient yourself with the Capitol dome as a landmark to the west, and
the rest of the city opens up naturally from this grid.
