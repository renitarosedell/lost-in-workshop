# Five Points & Neighbourhood Character

Five Points is the name of the intersection — and, by extension, the surrounding district —
where five streets converge at the southern edge of Raleigh's historic Drewry Hills and
Belmont neighbourhoods: Glenwood Avenue, Whitaker Mill Road, Fairview Road, Daniels
Street, and Cowper Drive all meet here in a small roundabout anchored by a fountain and a
cluster of neighbourhood businesses.

The Five Points district stretches roughly from the intersection northeast along Whitaker
Mill and southeast along Fairview, encompassing several blocks of mixed residential and
commercial uses. It feels like a village within the city — an effect that is partly
architectural (early twentieth-century bungalows and small commercial blocks rather than
glass towers) and partly social (the businesses here serve people who actually live in the
adjacent streets).

**What you'll find** at Five Points: Person Street Pizza (thin-crust, neighbourhood
institution), Irregardless Café (long-running vegetarian-friendly restaurant in the area),
Five Star Restaurant (diner classics with a devoted breakfast following), Legends Billiards
(neighbourhood bar), and Village District Coffee. The Rialto Theatre, a former cinema
turned event venue, anchors the southern edge of the district on Glenwood Avenue.

**The Village District** is the broader commercial area that extends from Five Points
south along Glenwood and east along Oberlin Road. It encompasses Whole Foods, REI, and a
collection of specialty shops — running gear, cookware, wine — in low-rise buildings set
back from the street with parking. It functions as the everyday commercial centre for the
upmarket residential neighbourhoods of Hayes Barton, Budleigh, and Drewry Hills.

**Neighbourhood character** in this part of Raleigh is defined by the houses. The streets
east of Five Points, particularly Hayes Barton and Budleigh, contain some of the most
carefully preserved early twentieth-century residential architecture in the state:
Colonial Revival and Tudor houses from the 1920s and 1930s on large lots with mature
trees. The 200-block of Canterbury Road is often cited in preservation guides.

**Getting to Five Points** from downtown: GoRaleigh Route 11 runs from Moore Square up
Glenwood Avenue through Glenwood South and terminates near Five Points. Rideshare is
twelve to fifteen minutes from Moore Square. Bikeshare is less convenient here — the
Capital Bikeshare network has limited coverage north of Peace Street; check the app before
planning a bike leg. Walking from Glenwood South takes about eight minutes.

**For workshop participants**, Five Points is primarily context rather than a game
location, but understanding it helps you orient within the northern part of the city. The
residential grid here was laid out on the old streetcar network lines, which is why the
streets radiate out from the Five Points hub in multiple directions rather than conforming
to the downtown grid. That streetcar history — the original Raleigh Electric Company lines
ran from 1891 until 1933 — shaped the character of many of Raleigh's mid-city
neighbourhoods.
