# Welcome to Raleigh

Raleigh, the capital of North Carolina, sits at the heart of one of the fastest-growing
metropolitan areas in the United States. Known as the "City of Oaks" for the white oak
trees that line its streets and parks, Raleigh blends the energy of a modern technology
hub with the unhurried warmth of a mid-size Southern city.

Founded in 1792 specifically to serve as a permanent state capital — a deliberate choice
made to avoid the political rivalries between the coastal city of New Bern and the inland
town of Hillsborough — Raleigh has spent more than two centuries building institutions: the
State Capitol, the university district, the research parks, and the cultural campus on
Bicentennial Plaza that today houses four world-class museums free of charge.

The city is the eastern anchor of the Research Triangle, a loosely defined region that
also encompasses Durham and Chapel Hill. The Triangle takes its name from the triangle
formed on a map by three major research universities: NC State University in Raleigh, Duke
University in Durham, and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. Those three
institutions, together with Research Triangle Park — the 7,000-acre campus of corporate
and government research labs between Raleigh and Durham — give the metro area an
unusually high density of PhDs, patents, and startups per square mile.

Raleigh's downtown has been remade over the past two decades. Empty warehouses have become
brewery taprooms and art galleries. Former tobacco buildings host co-working spaces. The
Fayetteville Street pedestrian corridor, once an ill-fated experiment in urban planning,
reopened to cars in 2006 and now anchors a thriving restaurant and nightlife scene that
connects the State Capitol to the convention centre and the performing arts campus.

For first-time visitors the city can feel deceptively quiet — spread out across a gently
rolling piedmont landscape with good tree cover and neighbourhoods that flow into one
another without sharp edges. It rewards slow exploration: a walk through Boylan Heights
yields craftsman bungalows and a bridge view of the downtown skyline; a turn through Five
Points delivers a village within the city; an afternoon in the Warehouse District connects
galleries, breweries, and live music in a single stroll.

The climate is genuinely four-season. Summers are warm and humid, with temperatures in the
high 80s Fahrenheit (low 30s Celsius). Springs and autumns are famously pleasant, the oaks
and maples turning brilliant in October. Winters are mild by northern standards but cold
enough for an occasional dusting of snow that reliably causes city-wide excitement.

Raleigh is proud of its food scene, which has grown from a handful of barbecue joints and
meat-and-three diners into a full-spectrum dining city with James Beard–nominated chefs,
acclaimed ramen shops, a thriving craft beer and cocktail culture, and farmers' markets
that supply the restaurants with North Carolina–grown ingredients.

Whether you are here for the workshop, for work, or just passing through, you will find a
city that takes its history seriously, invests in its future relentlessly, and still leaves
time to sit on a porch with a glass of sweet tea.
