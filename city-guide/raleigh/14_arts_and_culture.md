# Arts & Culture: NC Museum of Art and CAM Raleigh

Raleigh punches above its weight in the visual arts, a fact that surprises visitors who
expect a mid-size state capital to have at most a token art museum. The combination of
the NC Museum of Art and CAM Raleigh represents a genuine cultural asset.

**The North Carolina Museum of Art (NCMA)** sits on Blue Ridge Road in western Raleigh,
about 1.5 miles from downtown. Founded in 1947, it was the first museum in the nation
to receive significant state funding for the purchase of artworks — a fact the museum
is rightly proud of. Its permanent collection spans 5,000 years of art across Egyptian,
Greek, Roman, European, American, and African holdings.

The museum's permanent collection highlights include:
- A substantial European collection anchored by Rubens, Raphael, and Lippi.
- One of the strongest collections of ancient Mediterranean art in the South.
- A significant American collection with Hudson River School paintings and early American
  portraits.
- Rodin bronzes and a range of twentieth-century European works.

The museum's **West Building** (the original 1983 Lawrence-designed modernist box) holds
the permanent collection. The **East Building**, a stunning 2010 Thomas Phifer-designed
glass pavilion, hosts temporary exhibitions and public programming. Between the two
buildings is the **Ann and Jim Goodnight Museum Park**, a 164-acre outdoor sculpture
park where installations by Ai Weiwei, Thomas Sayre, and others are set into the
landscape. It is open for free, every day of the year, dawn to dusk.

Admission to the permanent collection is free. Temporary exhibitions have a fee. The
museum café is good; the museum store carries one of the better selections of art books
in the region.

**CAM Raleigh (Contemporary Art Museum)** is in the Warehouse District at 409 West
Martin Street, in a converted 1950s warehouse with a high-ceilinged white-box interior
that makes for excellent exhibition space. Its programming focuses on living artists with
national and international profiles, and it has a reputation for taking risks on
experimental work that the NCMA (understandably, given its institutional scale) does not.

CAM Raleigh is free and open Tuesday through Sunday. Its shop carries artist multiples,
books, and locally-designed goods. The museum's ground floor is also used for evening
events, talks, and the occasional art fair.

The museum's registry of past exhibitions, catalogued by curator number, served as the
source for the Museum Mile quest research code MUSEUMRUN88 — a reference to the museum's
88th registered show, which documented the history of the Warehouse District's industrial
past alongside contemporary responses.

**Getting to NCMA**: GoRaleigh Route 11 westbound from downtown to Blue Ridge Road, or
rideshare (about 10 minutes from downtown). The museum has free parking.

**Getting to CAM Raleigh**: Walk south from downtown on South Blount Street (~8 minutes
from Moore Square), or rideshare from anywhere downtown in under 5 minutes.
