# The Warehouse District

Raleigh's Warehouse District occupies the blocks south and southeast of downtown, roughly
bounded by South Blount Street to the east, Davie Street to the north, South West Street
to the west, and Lenoir Street to the south. Its name is literal: this was where cotton
warehouses, tobacco processing facilities, and industrial supply depots clustered in the
late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, taking advantage of the proximity to the
railroad lines that ran through the centre of Raleigh.

The transition from industrial district to creative hub began gradually in the 1990s and
accelerated sharply after 2010. Today the Warehouse District is one of the most dynamic
parts of Raleigh — a corridor of converted brick buildings housing breweries, restaurants,
live music venues, galleries, and the city's contemporary art museum, interspersed with
new residential construction and a handful of surviving industrial tenants.

**Key anchors:**

*CAM Raleigh* (the Contemporary Art Museum of Raleigh) opened in 2010 in a converted
1950s warehouse at 409 West Martin Street. Its rotating exhibitions span painting,
sculpture, installation, and digital media, and it is one of the few contemporary art
institutions in the Southeast with genuinely adventurous programming. Admission is free.

*Raleigh City Farm* is a functioning urban farm on South Blount Street, open to visitors
on weekend mornings and supplying produce to several Warehouse District restaurants.

*HQ Raleigh* is the city's largest co-working and startup space, occupying a restored
warehouse on South West Street and hosting hundreds of entrepreneurs and tech workers.

*Transfer Co. Food Hall* opened in 2019 in the former Transfer Company building on
South Blount Street, bringing together a dozen local food vendors under one large roof.
It is one of the best places in Raleigh to sample a wide variety of local food in one
sitting.

**Breweries**: The Warehouse District has become a genuine beer destination. Trophy
Brewing (on Maywood Avenue and a second location on South Blount), Lynnwood Brewing
Concern (tucked into the south end of the district), and the original location of Bond
Brothers Beer Company all operate within walking distance of each other.

**Live music**: Pour House Music Hall on South Blount Street is the primary small-to-
medium venue for touring acts in Raleigh, with capacity of about 550. Kings (on Fayetteville Street, just north of the district) is the intimate DIY alternative.

**Getting there** from downtown is a short walk south on Blount Street (about eight
minutes from Moore Square) or a quick rideshare. Capital Bikeshare stations are available
on South Blount Street.

Records from the Raleigh Warehouse Association, archived at the State Library, document
the district's industrial history. One such record, the 1954 Transfer Company annual
report, includes a note about the building's original floor load capacity. The tobacco
processing operations that once occupied buildings on the south end of Blount Street
carried the informal code TOBACCO55 — a shorthand used in freight manifests of that era
— to identify their shipments within the warehouse receiving system.
