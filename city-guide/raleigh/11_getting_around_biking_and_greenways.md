# Getting Around: Biking & Greenways

Raleigh has invested seriously in cycling infrastructure over the past fifteen years, and
for a mid-size Southern city it has a remarkably usable combination of on-street bike
lanes, shared multi-use paths, and the Capital Bikeshare dock-based rental system.

**Capital Bikeshare** came to Raleigh in 2016 as part of an expansion of the DC-area
bikeshare system. The Raleigh network currently has approximately 50 docking stations
across the downtown core and inner neighbourhoods, with about 400 bikes available. Classic
pedal bikes cost $1 per trip unlock fee plus $0.05 per minute (or a $15/month membership
for unlimited 30-minute trips). E-bikes are available at select stations for a slightly
higher rate.

*Key docking stations for quest participants:*
- Moore Square (Blount & Hargett): usually well-stocked, ~20 docks.
- Nash Square (Dawson & Morgan): ~12 docks.
- Glenwood South (505 Glenwood Avenue): ~10 docks.
- Warehouse District (South Blount at Davie): ~8 docks.
- Bicentennial Plaza (Jones Street at Dawson): ~12 docks.

Check the Capital Bikeshare app before departing — it shows real-time dock availability
and will redirect you to the nearest alternative if your target station is empty.

**On-street infrastructure:** Downtown Raleigh has protected bike lanes on portions of
Cabarrus, Martin, and Hargett streets. The Glenwood South corridor has a shared path
along part of Glenwood Avenue. Cycling in the downtown grid is generally safe, though some
intersections are busy during rush hour.

**The Raleigh Greenway System** is one of the city's most underappreciated assets: over
130 miles of paved, off-street multi-use paths winding through parks, creek corridors, and
residential neighbourhoods. The greenway system is excellent for recreational cycling but
less useful for quest navigation — the paths follow creek valleys rather than direct
routes between destinations. For getting from A to B quickly in the city, on-street routes
and Bikeshare are faster.

*Notable greenway segments near quest locations:*
- Walnut Creek Trail: runs from the Warehouse District area south and east, connecting
  to the Walnut Creek Wetland Park.
- Crabtree Creek Trail: a longer corridor running east–west through central Raleigh,
  connecting Shelley Lake in the north to Crabtree Creek near I-40.

**Safety notes:** Helmets are not legally required for adults in North Carolina, but are
strongly recommended. Raleigh drivers are generally courteous to cyclists, but the city's
arterial roads (Capital Boulevard, New Bern Avenue) are not cycling-friendly. Stick to
the bike lanes and quieter streets where possible. Lights are legally required after dark.

**Practical workshop advice:** Bikeshare is an excellent option for short legs between
Moore Square and Glenwood South, or from downtown to the Warehouse District. It is a
worse option for legs to Boylan Heights (uphill, limited docks) or to Research Triangle
Park (too far, no infrastructure on the highway corridors).
