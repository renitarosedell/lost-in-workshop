# The NC Biotech Center & Research Triangle Park

The North Carolina Biotechnology Center is the final destination of every Lost in Raleigh
quest, and it is a fitting one. The Biotech Center has been at the centre of North
Carolina's science and technology economy for four decades, and its campus in Research
Triangle Park embodies the ambitions of a state that bet on knowledge as its economic
future and largely won that bet.

**History:**
The NC Biotechnology Center was established in 1984 by the North Carolina General Assembly
as a private non-profit corporation with state funding — an unusual structure designed to
give it the flexibility of a private organisation with the public mission of a state
agency. It was among the first state-sponsored biotechnology development organisations in
the nation. Its founding was driven by the recognition that the Triangle universities had
extraordinary capabilities in molecular biology, pharmacology, and biomedical research,
and that the state needed an institution to translate that capability into economic activity.

Early investments went to supporting university research with state-of-the-art equipment,
training scientists in business and commercialisation skills, and connecting North Carolina
companies to capital and markets. Over the following decades those investments compounded:
North Carolina today has one of the largest concentrations of life sciences employment in
the United States, with major pharmaceutical and biotech facilities from companies
including Burroughs Wellcome (now GSK), Biogen, Novo Nordisk, and dozens of others
employing tens of thousands of people across the Triangle.

**The campus:**
The Biotech Center's main campus at 15 T.W. Alexander Drive consists of several
interconnected modern buildings on a landscaped campus of pine and hardwood forest.
The main building houses conference and event facilities, staff offices, and the
BioAccelerator business incubator.

Adjacent to the main campus are the Innovation Hall event spaces and the Frontier
co-working facility, which houses several dozen early-stage life sciences startups.
The Biotech Center offers these startups access to shared laboratory equipment, mentorship,
and the Biotech Center's network of industry and investor contacts.

**Research Triangle Park:**
The surrounding Research Triangle Park, of which the Biotech Center campus is a part,
is managed by the Research Triangle Foundation (RTF), a non-profit established in 1959.
The Foundation owns and leases land to the 300+ organisations in the park. Major
tenants include IBM, Cisco, NetApp, Credit Suisse operations, the US EPA Research
Triangle Park campus, and Research Triangle Institute International (RTI), one of the
largest independent research organisations in the world.

RTP is unusual among science parks in that it was deliberately designed without retail,
restaurants, or residential uses — a decision made in the 1950s that made economic
development sense at the time but has since created the campus's defining tension between
its success as a place of work and its weakness as a place to spend time. A large-scale
redevelopment initiative called The Hub is now underway on 140 acres in the centre of
the park, designed to add mixed-use development, public plazas, and an events venue.

**For workshop participants:**
The NC Biotech Center is where you are heading. Its conference facilities are world-class
for events of this scale, and the landscape around the campus — quiet roads through pine
forest, generous car parks, the occasional deer — is the Research Triangle at its most
characteristic.
