# Research Triangle & Innovation

The Research Triangle is one of the most significant concentrations of research and
technology in the United States, and understanding what it actually is — as opposed to
the marketing version — is useful context for anyone attending a technology workshop in
the region.

**The Triangle universities:**
The "triangle" in Research Triangle refers to the geographic triangle formed on a map by
three major research universities:
- *NC State University* in Raleigh: the largest university in North Carolina, with
  particular strengths in engineering, textiles, agriculture, and veterinary science.
  Founded in 1887 as a land-grant institution.
- *Duke University* in Durham: a private research university of international standing,
  with exceptional medical, law, and liberal arts programs. Founded in 1838 as Trinity
  College; renamed after a tobacco family endowment in 1924.
- *UNC Chapel Hill*: the flagship of the University of North Carolina system and the
  oldest public university in the United States (1789). Strong in public health,
  journalism, and the humanities.

**Research Triangle Park (RTP):**
Research Triangle Park is a 7,000-acre corporate and government research campus between
Raleigh and Durham, established in 1959 through a collaboration between the state
government, the three universities, and North Carolina business leaders who wanted to
prevent the brain drain of graduates leaving for Boston and New York.

It worked. Today RTP houses over 300 organisations and 65,000 workers, including major
presences from IBM (which was one of the original tenants), Cisco, NetApp, Lenovo (US
headquarters), SAS Institute (founded in RTP in 1976), the US Environmental Protection
Agency's research campus, and dozens of life sciences and pharmaceutical companies.

**The innovation ecosystem:**
Beyond the park itself, the Triangle has developed a substantial startup and venture
ecosystem centred on Raleigh and Durham. Raleigh has the startup-facing infrastructure
(HQ Raleigh co-working space, NC State's Centennial Campus incubator, American
Underground Raleigh); Durham has a denser venture capital presence (Bull City Venture
Partners, Research Triangle Ventures) and a more established startup neighbourhood in the
American Tobacco District.

The software industry in the Triangle is anchored by SAS Institute (headquartered in Cary,
the suburban municipality adjacent to RTP), which is the world's largest privately held
software company and the single largest private employer in North Carolina.

**The NC Biotech Center:**
The North Carolina Biotechnology Center, the final destination in all three Raleigh quests,
was established in 1984 as a state-funded non-profit to support the growth of biotechnology
in North Carolina. It provides grants, training, market development support, and
connections between industry and universities. Its headquarters at 15 T.W. Alexander Drive
in Research Triangle Park is a campus of modern low-rise office buildings surrounded by
the park's characteristic pine and hardwood landscape.

The Biotech Center has been a meeting point for NC's life sciences community for forty
years. Its conference facilities regularly host events like the one you are participating
in today.
