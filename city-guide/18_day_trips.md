Day Trips from the City

San Francisco's position makes it one of the best-located cities in the world for day trips. Within two hours in almost any direction there is something worth seeing.

### Napa Valley (1.5–2 hours)

The most famous wine region in the United States lies just over an hour north of the city. The valley is lined with wineries ranging from historic family estates to spectacular architectural showpieces. The Oxbow Public Market in Napa town is an excellent food hall. Highway 29 runs through the valley; the Silverado Trail on the eastern edge is more scenic and less trafficked.

### Sonoma & the Wine Country (1–1.5 hours)

Sonoma County is more varied than Napa and, many argue, more interesting. The Russian River Valley produces excellent Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. The town of Sonoma has a pleasant plaza and several good restaurants. Healdsburg is arguably the most sophisticated food and wine town in California.

### Muir Woods & Mount Tamalpais (45 minutes)

Muir Woods National Monument, just north of the Golden Gate, protects one of the few remaining old-growth coastal redwood groves accessible from a major city. The trees — some over 1,000 years old — require no explanation. Arrive early or take the shuttle from Sausalito (required on weekends). Combining Muir Woods with a hike on Mount Tamalpais makes for an excellent full-day outing.

### Point Reyes National Seashore (1.5 hours)

One of the most beautiful stretches of California coast, Point Reyes is a national seashore north of San Francisco with dramatic headlands, isolated beaches, a working dairy farming community, and excellent oysters from Drakes Bay. The Point Reyes Lighthouse is worth the considerable walk down and back up. Check whale watching conditions in winter.

### The Big Sur Coast (3 hours)

Technically more than a day trip, but an early start makes it feasible. Highway 1 south from San Francisco runs through some of the most spectacular coastal scenery in the world. Stop at the Bixby Creek Bridge, Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park, and McWay Falls. Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park is a must.

### Yosemite National Park (3.5–4 hours)

The crown jewel of the national park system, Yosemite Valley with its granite walls, waterfalls, and meadows is within reach for a long day trip or better yet an overnight. Spring is the best season for waterfalls; autumn for color; winter for solitude (and snow). Reservations are required for vehicle entry in peak season.

### Berkeley (30 minutes by BART)

The University of California campus is beautiful, particularly the Sather Tower and the Doe Library. The Gourmet Ghetto around Shattuck Avenue is where Chez Panisse operates. The Berkeley Bowl is the best grocery store in the Bay Area by some distance. The Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life at the Bancroft Library is exceptional.

### Oakland (20 minutes by BART)

Oakland is a city in its own right and deserves more than a day trip mention, but for the visitor based in San Francisco, Temescal, the Fruitvale neighborhood, Jack London Square, and the Oakland Museum of California are all worth crossing the bay for.
