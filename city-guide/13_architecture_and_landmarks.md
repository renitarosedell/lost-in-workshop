Architecture & Iconic Landmarks

San Francisco is one of the architecturally richest cities in the United States, with an unusual concentration of Victorian housing stock surviving the 1906 earthquake and a skyline that mixes eras dramatically.

### The Golden Gate Bridge

The most photographed bridge in the world, opened in 1938. Art Deco in style, International Orange in color (a custom shade chosen to complement the natural surroundings and remain visible in fog), and remarkable in its engineering context: it was the longest suspension bridge in the world for 27 years. Walking or cycling across it is one of the essential San Francisco experiences. Access from either the San Francisco or Marin County side; the Vista Point on the Marin side offers the classic full-span view.

### The Bay Bridge

The San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge is actually two bridges meeting at Yerba Buena Island. The western span was rebuilt after the 1989 earthquake and opened in 2013 with a striking asymmetric single-tower self-anchored suspension design. The Bay Lights installation — 25,000 LED lights on the western span — is visible from the Embarcadero on clear nights.

### Painted Ladies & Victorian Housing

San Francisco has the largest intact collection of Victorian-era houses in the United States, most of them built between 1850 and 1915. The "Painted Ladies" — a row of colorful Victorians facing Alamo Square — is the most photographed example, but the architecture extends across the Western Addition, the Castro, Noe Valley, and much of the older residential fabric of the city.

### Transamerica Pyramid

San Francisco's most distinctive skyscraper, completed in 1972. The tapered pyramid form was controversial at the time and is now a beloved part of the skyline.

### City Hall

The San Francisco City Hall, rebuilt after the 1906 earthquake and completed in 1915, is one of the finest Beaux-Arts buildings in the United States. The dome is taller than the US Capitol's. It is open to the public on weekdays.

### Coit Tower

A fluted concrete column on Telegraph Hill, built in 1933 and famously resembling (apparently unintentionally) a fire hose nozzle. The interior WPA murals are exceptional; the exterior view from the base is worth the hike up the Filbert Steps.

### Salesforce Tower

San Francisco's tallest building at 326 meters, completed in 2017. The Pelli Clarke Pelli-designed tower now dominates the skyline. The Salesforce Transit Center at its base is a more interesting piece of architecture, including a rooftop public park suspended above the street.

### Mission Dolores

The adobe church built in 1791 is the oldest building in San Francisco and the sixth of California's 21 missions. The attached cemetery contains the graves of many early San Francisco residents, including several Native American individuals whose names are recorded. The juxtaposition with the 1918 Basilica next door is striking.
