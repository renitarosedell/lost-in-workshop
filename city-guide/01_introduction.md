Introduction

San Francisco sits on the tip of a narrow peninsula, surrounded on three sides by water, and manages to feel like it belongs to an entirely different era from the rest of California. It is a city of forty-seven hills, three hundred microclimates, and one relentlessly original identity. Fog rolls in through the Golden Gate every afternoon as if scheduled. Victorian houses crowd hillsides that would defeat most city planners. And somewhere between the Mission's taquerias and the Financial District's glass towers, a city of under a million people punches harder than almost anywhere else on the planet.

San Francisco has always attracted people who did not quite fit elsewhere. The Gold Rush brought the restless. The 1960s brought the idealistic. The tech boom brought the ambitious. Each wave left its mark without erasing what came before, which is why the city feels layered in a way that rewards the curious visitor willing to walk beyond the obvious.

This guide covers the city comprehensively, from its neighborhoods and food scene to its cultural institutions and hidden corners. Whether you are here for a weekend or a month, San Francisco will give you more than you expect.
