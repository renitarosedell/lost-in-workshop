Where to Stay

San Francisco has no shortage of accommodation, from international luxury hotels to boutique independents to hostels. Where you stay substantially shapes your experience.

### Downtown & Union Square

The most convenient location for transit access and walking to many major sights. Large chain hotels dominate. Expect to pay more for less space. The St. Francis on Union Square is a San Francisco institution; the newer 1 Hotel San Francisco near Embarcadero is a strong choice for the environmentally inclined.

### The Embarcadero & Financial District

The Ferry Building end of the city is well-located for waterfront access and the BART/Muni connection. The Loews Regency San Francisco occupies the upper floors of a skyscraper with dramatic views.

### Hayes Valley & Civic Center

Hayes Valley has emerged as one of the better neighborhoods to base yourself: walkable, excellent dining and coffee, and well-connected by transit. Several boutique hotels operate here.

### The Mission

For visitors who want to be closer to where the city actually lives, the Mission offers guesthouses, boutique properties, and Airbnbs at more reasonable prices than downtown. It is particularly well-located for the food and bar scene.

### Fisherman's Wharf

Practical for bay access and Alcatraz ferries, and the Argonaut Hotel here is genuinely appealing. Just know you will be walking a fair distance to most of the city's more interesting neighborhoods.

### Budget Options

HI San Francisco Fisherman's Wharf and HI San Francisco City Center are well-run hostels. The latter, near the Civic Center, is particularly central.

### Useful Tips

- Hotel rates vary enormously by day of week and season. Weekdays during tech conference season can be punishing.
- Parking at hotels is almost always expensive. Factor this in if arriving by car.
- Fog and wind affect the western half of the city significantly. If you mind cold, grey mornings, stay east of Divisadero.
