Parks & the Outdoors

For a dense urban environment, San Francisco has an extraordinary amount of green space, much of it within easy reach of any neighborhood.

### Golden Gate Park

At 412 hectares, Golden Gate Park is larger than Central Park and stretches from the Panhandle to the Pacific Ocean. It contains the de Young Museum, the California Academy of Sciences, the Japanese Tea Garden (the oldest public Japanese garden in the United States), the Botanical Garden, two windmills, multiple lakes, a bison paddock, and an archery range. It is also where residents exercise, picnic, and play on every dry weekend.

The park was engineered from sand dunes beginning in 1870 by landscape architect William Hammond Hall and his successor John McLaren, who spent 53 years tending it.

### The Presidio

The former military base at the northern tip of the peninsula is now a national park within the city. The Presidio includes forests, beaches, hiking trails, a golf course, and some of the best views of the Golden Gate Bridge. The Presidio Trust has converted many of the historic military buildings into restaurants and visitor facilities.

### Crissy Field

The tidal marsh and lawn along the northern waterfront between the Marina and the Presidio. A popular running, cycling, and kite-flying destination with unobstructed views of the Golden Gate Bridge.

### Dolores Park

Mission Dolores Park is the city's most social outdoor space. On a sunny weekend afternoon it is packed with every demographic the city contains. The view of the downtown skyline from the upper sections of the park is excellent. Bring food from one of the nearby taquerias or Bi-Rite Creamery next door.

### Twin Peaks

The highest accessible hills in the city, with a paved summit area offering a 360-degree panorama of the entire bay. Best visited at dusk.

### Lands End

A coastal trail in the Richmond District with views of the Golden Gate Bridge, dramatic rock formations, and the ruins of the Sutro Baths. One of the most spectacular short hikes in the city.

### Ocean Beach

The long, windswept Pacific shore running along the western edge of the city. Not a swimming beach — the currents are dangerous — but excellent for walking, surfing (experienced surfers only), and watching the sunset.

### Mount Tamalpais

Technically in Marin County, but within 45 minutes of downtown San Francisco, Mount Tamalpais offers hiking, mountain biking, and views from the summit across the entire bay area on clear days.
