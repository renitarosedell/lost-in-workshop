Practical Information

### Weather

San Francisco's weather is famously idiosyncratic. Mark Twain may or may not have said that the coldest winter he ever spent was a summer in San Francisco, but the observation is apt enough. The city's microclimates mean that while the Mission basks in sunshine, the Sunset may be under a marine layer. The warmest months are September and October; July and August are often cool and foggy. Bring layers regardless of the time of year.

### Safety

San Francisco is a generally safe city for visitors but has visible social challenges, particularly around the Tenderloin district and parts of SoMa. Exercise normal urban awareness and be alert in these areas, particularly at night. The Tenderloin is centrally located and many visitors pass through or near it; use common sense and stay on main streets.

### Money

San Francisco is an expensive city. Budget at least $25–35 per person for a casual lunch, $60–100 for a mid-range dinner, and significantly more for fine dining. Hotels run from around $120 per night for budget options to $400+ for mid-range. Many restaurants include service charges; check your bill before adding a tip.

ATMs are widely available. Most businesses accept contactless payment; cash is less necessary here than in many other cities.

### Connectivity

Cell coverage in the city is generally excellent. Free Wi-Fi is available in most cafes and the public library system. The SFO airport has reliable free Wi-Fi throughout.

### Tipping

Tipping is standard in San Francisco and expected. 18–22% is the norm at restaurants; 15% is on the low end. Tipping is also customary for taxi and rideshare drivers, hotel staff, tour guides, and baristas (though at a lower rate).

### Healthcare

San Francisco has excellent medical facilities. UCSF (University of California San Francisco) operates several hospital campuses and is one of the leading academic medical centers in the world. Emergency care is available at multiple hospitals; the UCSF Medical Center at Parnassus and Zuckerberg San Francisco General are the main public hospitals.

Pharmacies include Walgreens (numerous locations) and CVS.

### Useful Apps

- **Muni/BART**: Google Maps or Apple Maps handle transit routing reliably. Clipper card can be added to Apple Wallet.
- **Parking**: SpotHero or ParkWhiz for pre-booking, or the SFMTA parking meter app.
- **Dining**: Resy and Tock for reservations; Yelp remains widely used for local restaurant research.
- **Rideshare**: Uber and Lyft are both active.

### Emergency Numbers

- Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): **911**
- Non-emergency police: **415-553-0123**
- SF Visitor Information Center (Hallidie Plaza, near Powell BART): open daily

### Time Zone

Pacific Time (UTC–8 in winter, UTC–7 during Daylight Saving Time, which runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November).

### Electricity

Standard US 110V / 60Hz with Type A and Type B outlets. European and other visitors will need a plug adapter and potentially a voltage converter for sensitive electronics.

### Language

English is the primary language. San Francisco has large Spanish-speaking, Cantonese-speaking, and Mandarin-speaking populations; in the Mission and Richmond respectively, Spanish and Chinese are commonly heard. Most businesses and all official services operate in English.
