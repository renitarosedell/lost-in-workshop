Bars & Nightlife

San Francisco's nightlife is smaller in scale than Los Angeles or New York, but it punches above its weight in quality and originality.

### Cocktail Bars

The city has a strong cocktail culture with a preference for well-sourced spirits and seasonally adjusted menus.

**The Interval at Long Now** at Fort Mason is one of the most interesting bar environments in any city: situated inside the headquarters of the Long Now Foundation, surrounded by a 10,000-year library, with exceptional cocktails and natural wine. Go for the atmosphere as much as the drinks.

**Comstock Saloon** in North Beach serves classic cocktails in a beautiful 1907 bar. The Martinez — precursor to the Martini, invented nearby — is on the menu.

**ABV** on Valencia Street is a neighborhood cocktail bar done right: no pretension, great drinks, excellent snacks.

**Trick Dog** in the Mission rotates its entire menu concept every six months and has been doing so since 2013.

### Dive Bars

San Francisco has excellent dive bars. **Zeitgeist** in the Mission has a massive outdoor beer garden, cheap beer, and no apologies. **Specs' Twelve Adler Museum Café** in North Beach is a North Beach institution: dark, crowded with maritime memorabilia, and absolutely non-negotiable.

### Wine Bars

The city's proximity to Napa, Sonoma, and smaller appellations like Livermore and Santa Cruz Mountains gives it an outstanding wine bar scene. **Birba** in the Castro and **Ordinaire** in Temescal (Oakland, but close) are worth knowing.

### The Club Scene

Electronic music clubs are concentrated in SoMa. **1015 Folsom** is the large-format venue; **Public Works** in the Mission hosts a range of events from indie nights to techno. The LGBTQ+ club scene around the Castro and SoMa is extensive.

### Late Night

San Francisco's last call is 2 AM, consistent with California state law. This means nightlife starts earlier than in many international cities. Plan accordingly.
