Hidden Gems & Local Tips

The well-known attractions of San Francisco are well-known for reasons — but the city rewards exploration beyond the obvious. Here are things visitors less often find.

### The Filbert Steps

The steep wooden staircase on the east side of Telegraph Hill, descending through private gardens to Levi Strauss Plaza, is one of the most beautiful urban walks in any American city. Wild parrots (a feral colony of cherry-headed conures) live in the trees around Coit Tower.

### The Wave Organ

At the tip of the jetty enclosing the Marina's small boat harbor, the Wave Organ is a sound sculpture built from organ pipes and found materials. The waves pumping through the pipes create ambient musical sounds. It is strange and wonderful, particularly at high tide.

### Balmy Alley

A single block in the Mission between 24th and 25th Streets, Balmy Alley has been covered in political murals since the 1970s. It is one of the densest concentrations of outdoor political art in the city, and it changes over time.

### The Columbarium

The Neptune Society Columbarium in the Richmond District is a Victorian-era crematorium rotunda, the only surviving building from the era when San Francisco's cemeteries occupied what is now the Sunset and Richmond Districts. The ornate niches contain urns and mementos going back to the 1890s. Free to visit and completely surreal.

### 16th Avenue Tiled Steps

In the Inner Sunset, the Moraga Steps (at 16th and Moraga) are covered in an elaborate mosaic running from the street to the hilltop park above. A collaborative community art project that took years to complete.

### The Buena Vista Cafe

At the north end of Hyde Street near the cable car turnaround, the Buena Vista claims to have introduced Irish Coffee to the United States in 1952. Whether or not that is strictly true, they make them in enormous quantities and they are excellent.

### Anchor Brewing

San Francisco's Anchor Steam Beer is one of the original craft beers of the American craft brewing revival. The brewery, now over 150 years old, offers tours and is housed in a beautiful historic building. The taproom has excellent beer and genuine San Francisco provenance.

### The Randall Museum

A small natural history museum in the Corona Heights neighborhood with a working model train layout, live animals, and changing exhibits. Beloved by locals and largely unknown to visitors.

### Stow Lake

Inside Golden Gate Park, Stow Lake has a rowboat rental concession that has been operating for decades. Renting a boat and circling Strawberry Hill island is one of the genuinely relaxing things you can do in the park.

### Drinking in the Sunset

The Outer Sunset along Irving Street and Judah Street has a cluster of bars and coffee shops that represent a different, quieter San Francisco. Outerlands is the neighborhood's most loved restaurant; The Riptide is the dive bar of choice, a few blocks from the ocean.
