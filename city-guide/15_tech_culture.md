Tech Culture & Innovation

No honest city guide to San Francisco can ignore the tech industry that has reshaped it so dramatically.

### Silicon Valley and the City

Strictly speaking, Silicon Valley refers to the South Bay corridor running from San Jose through Sunnyvale, Mountain View, and Palo Alto. Most of the large tech companies — Apple, Google, Meta, Intel, HP — are headquartered there, not in the city. But starting around 2010, a wave of companies chose San Francisco proper as their base: Twitter, Uber, Lyft, Airbnb, Salesforce, Stripe, OpenAI, and dozens more.

### The Culture

The tech culture of San Francisco is visible throughout the city: in the coffee shops full of people on laptops, in the neighborhoods it has transformed, in the private shuttle buses that run employees to the South Bay campuses, and in the conversations at almost any bar or dinner table. It is a culture that values disruption, scale, and speed, and it sits uneasily alongside the city's older traditions of labor activism, artistic experimentation, and social welfare politics.

### Computer History Museum

Located in Mountain View, a 40-minute drive south, the Computer History Museum is one of the finest museums in the United States on any subject. The permanent exhibit covers the full arc of computing history from the abacus to the internet with extraordinary artifacts and clear storytelling. Essential for anyone interested in the technology that has shaped the modern world.

### Exploratorium & Science Culture

The Exploratorium (see Museums chapter) has been influencing science education and thinking about public understanding of science for over 50 years. It is a product of a Bay Area culture that takes science seriously as a public endeavor.

### The Long Now Foundation

The Long Now Foundation, founded by Stewart Brand and others in 1996, builds infrastructure for long-term thinking: a 10,000-year clock inside a mountain in Nevada, a library of endangered languages, and The Interval bar and café at Fort Mason. It represents a strand of San Francisco intellectual culture that thinks carefully about timescales beyond the quarterly earnings report.
