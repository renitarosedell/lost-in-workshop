The Bay & the Waterfront

The bay defines San Francisco in ways that no other geographical feature could. It shapes the weather, the light, the economy, and the imagination of the city.

### The Ferry Building

The 1898 Ferry Building at the foot of Market Street was the main entry point to San Francisco for decades before the bridges were built. Restored in 2003, it now houses the Ferry Building Marketplace with permanent stalls from local food producers and a world-class farmers' market on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday mornings. Eating your way through the Ferry Building on a Saturday morning is one of San Francisco's genuine pleasures.

### Alcatraz

The federal penitentiary that operated on Alcatraz Island from 1934 to 1963 housed some of the most notorious criminals in American history, including Al Capone and Robert Stroud, the "Birdman of Alcatraz." Ferries depart from Pier 33, and the audio tour (voiced by former guards and inmates) is remarkably well produced. Book in advance — tours sell out weeks ahead in summer.

### Angel Island

The largest island in the bay, Angel Island is a state park accessible by ferry from the Ferry Building or Tiburon. It served as the "Ellis Island of the West" — an immigration processing station for hundreds of thousands of immigrants, particularly from Asia, between 1910 and 1940. The hiking and cycling are excellent, and the views of the city from the summit are extraordinary.

### The Embarcadero

The waterfront boulevard running from AT&T Park (home of the San Francisco Giants) to Fisherman's Wharf is one of the most pleasant urban waterfronts in California. The elevated freeway that once blocked it was damaged in the 1989 earthquake and subsequently demolished; the current boulevard, with its historic streetcar line, was the result.

### Pier 39 & Sea Lions

In 1989, following the Loma Prieta earthquake, a large colony of California sea lions took up residence on the floating docks of Pier 39. They have been there ever since. At peak season the colony can reach 1,700 individuals. They are loud, pungent, and utterly compelling.

### Sausalito & Tiburon

Across the Golden Gate, the small towns of Sausalito and Tiburon are accessible by ferry and offer a different perspective on the bay. Sausalito has a significant artist community, excellent restaurants on the waterfront, and houseboats in Richardson Bay. Tiburon is quieter and more residential.
