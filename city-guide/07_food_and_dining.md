Food & Dining

San Francisco has one of the most serious food cultures of any city in the world. The combination of extraordinary local produce from the Central Valley, a fishing industry, a wine region next door, and wave after wave of immigrant cuisine has produced something remarkable. You can eat exceptionally well here at nearly every price point.

### The Food Identity

The concept of "California cuisine" — seasonal, local, produce-forward, and influenced by Asian and Latin American ingredients — was largely born here. Alice Waters' Chez Panisse across the bay in Berkeley established the template in 1971, and its influence runs through the entire dining scene. San Francisco restaurants tend to be ingredient-led rather than technique-obsessed.

### Must-Try Food Experiences

**Sourdough bread** is a city landmark in edible form. The particular strain of wild yeast that gives San Francisco sourdough its distinctive tang has been cultivated here for over 150 years. Tartine Bakery in the Mission produces what many consider the best loaf in the city; arrive early or expect a wait.

**Mission burritos** are a specific San Francisco invention: a large flour tortilla wrapped tightly around rice, beans, meat, salsas, and guacamole. They bear little resemblance to what is called a burrito elsewhere. La Taqueria, El Farolito, and Taqueria Cancún are the main points of debate.

**Dungeness crab** is in season from November through June. Fresh from the boats at Fisherman's Wharf, steamed and eaten at a picnic table, it is one of the best things you can eat in this city.

**Dim sum** in the Richmond or Chinatown is an institution. Yank Sing in SoMa is the elegant option; Good Luck Dim Sum on Clement Street is the neighbourhood standby.

### Dining Neighborhoods

**The Mission** is the most densely interesting neighborhood for food, with everything from Michelin-starred tasting menus (Lazy Bear) to some of the best tacos on the West Coast.

**The Richmond (Clement Street and Geary Boulevard)** is the city's most underrated food neighborhood: Vietnamese pho, Burmese tea leaf salad, Russian dumplings, Taiwanese beef noodle soup, and excellent Chinese seafood all within a few blocks.

**Hayes Valley** has a concentration of excellent mid-range restaurants suited to a civilized dinner before a Symphony or Opera performance.

**North Beach** for Italian. It is touristy in parts but the pasta and the antipasti are genuine.

### Price Levels

San Francisco is not a cheap dining city, but value exists. Taquerias, ramen shops, dim sum houses, and the city's many Vietnamese restaurants offer genuine quality at accessible prices. Tipping is standard at around 18–22%.

### Reservations

For anything above the casual tier, reservations are essential, often weeks in advance. Resy and Tock are the dominant platforms. Some restaurants (Lazy Bear, Bix) require reservations long in advance.
