Shopping

San Francisco is not a shopping capital in the mode of New York or Paris, but it has a distinctive retail character shaped by independent booksellers, vintage clothing, local designers, and specialty food.

### Books

**City Lights Bookstore** in North Beach is required. Founded by Lawrence Ferlinghetti in 1953, it remains an independent bookstore and publisher of genuine literary importance. The poetry room upstairs, where Ginsberg's *Howl* was first published, is a cultural landmark.

**Green Apple Books** in the Inner Richmond is a sprawling, wonderfully disorganized multi-floor used and new bookshop. One of the best bookshops in the United States.

**Dog Eared Books** on Valencia Street in the Mission is smaller but carefully curated.

### Vintage & Clothing

Haight Street (both Upper and Lower) is lined with vintage shops. **Wasteland** and **Buffalo Exchange** are the larger chains; the smaller independent shops yield better finds.

**Heath Ceramics** has its factory and flagship store in Sausalito and a retail presence in San Francisco. Their tableware is a distinctly Bay Area aesthetic object.

### Food & Specialty

**Ferry Building Marketplace** houses a permanent collection of the Bay Area's best food producers: Acme Bread, Cowgirl Creamery, Blue Bottle Coffee, and dozens more. The Saturday farmers' market outside is one of the finest in California.

**Bi-Rite Market** on 18th Street in the Mission is a small grocery that functions as a community institution. The selection of local and artisan products is exceptional.

### Hayes Valley

The most pleasant neighborhood for a concentrated shopping afternoon: independent clothing, home goods, jewelry, and chocolate (Dandelion Chocolate has a café and factory here).
